# NeuralSS: open, hardware-agnostic neural reconstruction for games

Technical research plan for an open neural rendering and reconstruction stack that combines **temporal super-resolution** and **low-latency frame generation**, aims at native-quality output in static scenes and heavy motion, and runs on dedicated and integrated GPUs through vendor-neutral APIs.

## 1. Objective

Build an original, open and hardware-agnostic replacement for DLSS / FSR / XeSS-class reconstruction:

1. **Temporal super-resolution.** Reconstructs frames from jittered low-resolution renders plus engine metadata (motion vectors, depth, exposure, reactive / transparency masks, normals where available).
   - Primary ratio 3x (640x360 to 1920x1080, matching FSR's Ultra Performance ratio); 2x and 1.5x also validated.
   - Must be stable under heavy motion, recover detail in static scenes, and handle disocclusions, foliage, particles, transparencies, thin geometry and text.
2. **Frame generation.** Minimises input latency rather than maximising frames per second.
   - Interpolation and extrapolation (with late-latched camera input).
   - UI-correct: never interpolates or hallucinates HUD, subtitles, menus or pause screens.
   - Safe on camera cuts.
3. **Deployment.** Both networks reduce to plain 3x3 convolution chains plus warps, exportable through ONNX to DirectML, Vulkan compute (ncnn), Metal and TensorRT, with tiers from integrated GPUs to high-end discrete GPUs.

We do not reproduce DLSS, FSR or XeSS internals. The design draws on public research and open documentation (sections 3 and 20), and on what the open FidelityFX documentation says an engine must provide.

### 3.3 What we take from this

- **Engine signals.** All production systems rely on jitter, motion vectors, depth and separated UI. NeuralSS consumes the same signals and degrades gracefully when they are missing.
- **Speed.** Fast systems reuse history (TSR) and split per-pair from per-frame work (FG).
- **Latency.** Interpolation adds latency by construction; vendors offset it with Reflex, Anti-Lag 2 and XeLL. Extrapolation (ExtraSS, GFFE) and late-latched warping (Frame Warp) avoid holding frames. NeuralSS supports both and reports their latency separately (section 10).
- **Hardware.** Vendor ML paths are tied to tensor, XMX or RDNA 4 hardware; DP4a and plain FP16 compute are the cross-vendor path.

## 4. Architecture

### 4.1 Per-frame pipeline (engine integration target)

```text
engine renders jittered LR colour + depth + motion vectors (+ reactive mask, exposure)
  -> TSR step (LR space): history weight + residual, pixel shuffle to HR, history buffers updated
  -> post-processing / tone mapping at HR
  -> [optional] FG: interpolation (holds the real frame) or extrapolation (holds nothing, late camera latch)
  -> UI composited on every displayed frame (real and generated)
  -> paced present on a variable-refresh display
```

The TSR state (previous output, a small hidden state and the previous depth) lives in ping-pong buffers. It is reset on scene cuts, resolution changes and camera teleports.

### 4.2 Temporal super-resolution network

Inputs are at render resolution `h x w`; the output is `s` times larger.

| Input                                                                   | Channels | Source in training                                                 |
| :---------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Jittered colour (one sample per pixel, Halton 2,3 offset)               | 3        | Synthetic renderer, or jittered single-sample reads of real frames |
| Depth, normalised inverse-depth style `1 / (1 + d / median)`            | 1        | TartanAir, GameIR, synthetic layers                                |
| Normals derived from depth (central differences)                        | 3        | Derived on the fly                                                 |
| Motion vectors, current to previous frame, render pixels                | 2        | Exact (synthetic, TartanAir, Sintel) or RAFT (video)               |
| Depth disocclusion cue `abs(d - warp(d_prev)) / d`                      | 1        | Derived                                                            |
| Reactive mask (transparent effects without motion vectors)              | 1        | Synthetic particles                                                |
| Exposure (log2 gain) and jitter offset                                  | 1 + 2    | Augmentation and sequence                                          |
| Flags: depth available, motion quality (1 exact, 0.5 estimated, 0 none) | 2        | Per sample                                                         |
| Previous output warped by upsampled motion vectors, space-to-depth      | 3 s^2    | Recurrent                                                          |
| `abs(avgpool(warped history) - colour)`                                 | 3        | Recurrent                                                          |
| Warped recurrent hidden state                                           | 8 to 32  | Recurrent                                                          |

**Trunk.** RepConv blocks: 3x3 + 1x1 + identity + fixed Sobel / Laplacian filters with a learnable 1x1 projection, after [ECBSR](https://github.com/xindongzhang/ECBSR) (Apache-2.0). They are folded into one 3x3 kernel on every forward pass (online re-parameterisation), so training costs one convolution per block and deployment is a plain chain. The 1x1 and edge-projection weights start at zero: with PyTorch's default initialisation every branch adds gain, and a NumPy simulation of the trunk at initialisation (an agent-local script, not kept in the repository) grows activations about 1.9x per block, to about 7e3 at the end of the 15-block teacher trunk on inputs in [0, 1] and enough to overflow FP16 on real inputs; with zero-initialised side branches the maximum stays near 2 for every tier. An optional U-Net level at half resolution (strided convolution down, pixel shuffle up) enlarges the receptive field for fast motion and disocclusions.

**Motion without engine data (3 November 2026).** Every tier has a built-in IFNet motion estimator (section 21, three flow levels at 1/4, 1/2 and full low resolution) that computes the motion vectors from the previous and current colour frames whenever a sample has none (`mv_quality` 0); its result enters the trunk with `mv_quality` 0.25. An input-kind channel (1 = jittered render, 0 = video) tells the trunk which degradation it sees. At inference the estimator is skipped when the engine supplies motion vectors.

**Output.** `out = a * warped_history + (1 - a) * jitter-aware bilinear upsample + residual`. The per-pixel history weight `a` and the residual are predicted at low resolution and unfolded by pixel shuffle; all tiers but `igpu` then add a two-layer 3x3 refinement at output resolution (zero-initialised), aimed at the pixel-shuffle dot pattern seen in the run of 24 October 2026 (hypothesis until measured).

- This is a learned version of temporal accumulation with rectification: `a` is driven toward 0 at disocclusions and particles and toward 1 on static content, where jittered samples accumulate toward native detail.
- The first frame after a reset uses `a = 0`.

### 4.3 Frame-generation network (redesigned 3 November 2026, section 21)

- **Inputs.** Real frames I0 (t = 0) and I1 (t = 1); any target time tau: 0 < tau < 1 interpolates (tau = 1/3, 2/3 for 3x multi-frame generation, and so on), tau = 1.5 extrapolates.
- **Motion.** An IFNet (RIFE 4.26's design): a per-frame 4-channel feature encoder, then five flow blocks at 1/16 to full resolution, each refining the flows target -> I0 and target -> I1, a blend logit and 8 feature channels. It needs only the two colour frames, so it works on ordinary video. The `high` tier is RIFE 4.26's network and loads its pre-trained weights; smaller tiers are narrower with fewer residual convolutions and inherit leading channel slices. Each tier runs the flow network at no more than a set number of image lines (576 for `igpu` and `low`, 1152 for `mid` and `high`), halving taller frames, so at 1080p `igpu` and `low` estimate flow at 540 lines (section 22; until the runs of 2 to 3 November 2026 they always halved).
- **Engine data (optional).** Linear-motion priors from the motion vector of I1 (I1 -> I0), `F(tau->0) = tau * M1` and `F(tau->1) = (tau - 1) * M1`, the camera-only flow from the target to I1 (latest camera input), depth of I1 and availability flags enter every flow block as extra input channels with zero initial weight, so a fresh `high` model reproduces RIFE 4.26 exactly (checked to 3e-6 against RIFE's own code) and fine-tuning learns how much to trust each signal. This replaces the old design, in which the prior was the starting flow and the network could not estimate motion without it.
- **Output.** A half-resolution head on the warped frames, blend weight and IFNet features predicts a residual and the UI / static-overlay logit: `out = u * I1 + (1 - u) * (m * warp(I0) + (1 - m) * warp(I1) + residual)`. Pixels classified as UI are copied from the newest real frame instead of being interpolated.
- **Per-pair and per-frame split.** The feature encoder depends on one frame only, so a running pipeline computes it once per real frame; the flow blocks and head run per generated frame (DLSS 4 splits its network the same way, section 3.1).
- **Cuts.** A cut detector bypasses generation and repeats the newest real frame: luma-histogram distance plus motion-compensated difference when the engine supplies motion vectors, histogram distance alone otherwise, each with a threshold calibrated for 1 percent false alarms (section 22).

### 4.4 Teacher networks

- **TSR teacher.** The same architecture at 64 channels, 8 blocks and a 6-block U-Net level. It trained normally in the runs of 20 to 21 October 2026 (33.72 dB validation PSNR-Y) and 24 October 2026 (33.83 dB, section 7.1). Distillation from it gave no measurable gain in either run, so from 3 November 2026 the students train without it and the teacher is the architecture's quality ceiling.
- **FG teacher.** Until 24 October 2026 a privileged teacher that also saw the true target frame. From 3 November 2026 the teacher is the `high` tier fine-tuned from RIFE 4.26: it sees only what a deployed model sees, is deployable on high-end GPUs and distils its flows and outputs into the smaller tiers.

## 5. Dataset strategy

### 5.1 Principles

1. **High-resolution sources only as targets.** Low-resolution inputs are generated (jittered render-like sampling), except where a native low-resolution render exists (GameIR 720p) for generalisation tests.
2. **Game-rendering metadata wherever it exists.** Exact flow, occlusion and depth (TartanAir, Sintel), depth and native multi-resolution renders (GameIR), plus a synthetic layered renderer that produces every engine signal exactly.
3. **Natural video for motion diversity**, with RAFT teacher motion marked as estimated.
4. **One manifest for both tasks**, with leakage groups and perceptual deduplication across every source (section 6).

### 5.3 Pipeline (`neural-data-pipeline` notebook, `nss_pipeline.py`)

1. **Discovery.**
   - Kaggle: a capped randomised directory walk, listing directories completely so sequences are never split. Degraded copies (LR, bicubic, blurred, `x2`/`x3`/`x4`) and non-colour passes are dropped by path token.
   - Hugging Face: HTTPS with retries. TartanAir is read member by member from remote zips with HTTP range requests; GameIR tars are streamed and stopped early; Vimeo-1080p parquet shards are unpacked into MP4 files. Both access patterns were checked against the live repositories on 5 October 2026.
2. **Normalisation.** RGB uint8 frames; depth to an inverse-depth style code; flow to engine motion-vector semantics.
3. **Leakage groups.** Every item gets one: Kaggle video folder or video, Sintel scene family, TartanAir environment, GameIR town, Vimeo-1080p video, still file.
4. **Quality filter.** Rejects items whose keyframes are smaller than 256 px, flat (grey standard deviation under 8), more than 35 percent letterboxed, or blurry (Laplacian variance under 20 for stills, 5 for video); sequences are judged on the median of their keyframes. Rejected items are kept in the manifest as `filtered` (151 of 12,300 in the run of 18 October 2026: 101 letterboxed, 47 blurry, 22 flat, counting items with several reasons once per reason).
5. **Deduplication and split.** Section 6. The output is `nss_manifest.parquet` with URIs that resolve on Kaggle (`kaggle:<ref>|path`, `data:path`, `mp4:path#frame`).

The two training notebooks load this manifest from an attached notebook output, or rebuild a smaller one inline with the same code.

### 5.4 Temporal sequences and motion-vector semantics

- All windows use the engine convention: for each pixel of frame k, the displacement to frame k-1.
- TartanAir and Sintel provide forward flow (frame k to k+1). Their windows are played **backwards**, so this forward flow becomes exactly the engine vector, and the occlusion mask becomes the disocclusion mask.
- Video windows get RAFT motion with a forward-backward consistency mask.
- Frame-generation quads (four consecutive frames) store teacher flows 2 to 0 (the newest real frame's motion vector), 1 to 0 and 1 to 2 (interpolation targets), and 3 to 2 (extrapolation target).

### 5.5 Synthetic game-like renderer (`nss_synth.py`)

Rendered on the GPU from cached high-resolution stills:

- **Scene.** A background plane and a foreground layer with a procedural alpha: solid blobs, wires one to two texels wide, and fences of thin bars.
- **Motion.** Each layer has its own continuous affine camera path. Speed classes are static, slow (0.3 to 3 px/frame), medium (3 to 10) and fast (10 to 28), with rotation, zoom, acceleration and optional camera reversal.
- **Effects.** Alpha-blended particles that write no motion vectors (like real engines) and set a reactive mask; world-space text; exposure changes.
- **Overlays (FG).** Screen-space HUD tiles, subtitle changes, full-screen menus and scene cuts.
- **Rendering.** Low-resolution frames take one jittered sample per pixel from the full-resolution texture with no mip filtering, so minification produces real aliasing on thin geometry. Targets use 4 samples per pixel (rotated grid).
- **Ground truth.** Motion vectors, intermediate flows, disocclusion masks and depth are exact because every pixel's layer and trajectory are known.

**Hypothesis.** This closes most of the gap left by the lack of open game G-buffer data for motion, disocclusion and aliasing behaviour. It does not model lighting effects such as specular highlights, reflections and shadows (section 15).

## 6. Deduplication and leakage strategy

Copy detection runs in two stages, because global similarity alone cannot tell the same picture from a similar picture (two cherry-tree photos, two skylines or two frames of one game all have high DINOv2 cosine similarity). The first Kaggle run used global similarity only (pHash Hamming 14, DINOv2 cosine 0.75): false edges chained 8,354 items into one component that touched a test item, and 8,150 training items (72 percent) were dropped.

### 6.2 Limitations and leakage risks

1. Duplicates are judged on keyframes. Two sequences that share only mid-sequence content can slip through.
2. Similarity thresholds are global; per-source calibration would be tighter.
3. Strong augmentations beyond the calibration set (heavy colour grading, large overlays, extreme crops under 50 percent) are missed.
4. Scenes that are related but different, such as other trajectories in the same TartanAir environment, other Sintel shots of the same set, or REDS clips from the same city walk, are kept together only through the coarse leakage groups (environment, scene family). Groups that are too fine would leak.
5. Synthetic training scenes reuse textures from training stills only. Test synthetic scenes use test stills (held-out game frames and DIV2K validation images).
6. RAFT was trained on Sintel and other flow datasets ([RAFT](https://github.com/princeton-vl/RAFT)), so motion estimated on Sintel-like content is optimistic. Sintel test sequences use exact flow, not RAFT.

## 7. Temporal super-resolution training strategy

- **Data mix per step.**
  - About 60 percent synthetic sequences (exact everything).
  - About 40 percent real windows: TartanAir and Sintel exact; REDS, Vimeo, Vimeo-1080p and GameIR with RAFT motion and `mv_quality = 0.5`.
  - Real windows get motion-consistent flips and exposure augmentation.
  - From 3 November 2026, two input kinds: 30 percent of synthetic and 60 percent of real samples become plain video (anti-aliased bicubic, bilinear or area downscale, Gaussian pre-blur up to sigma 0.6, noise up to 2/255, 8-bit quantisation, no jitter, depth or motion vectors; 15 percent of them keep estimated motion vectors); render samples lose their motion vectors with probability 0.25 and depth with 0.3.
- **Unroll.** Six frames per sequence (truncated back-propagation through the recurrence), 192x192 output patches (64x64 input at 3x). From 3 November 2026 every fourth step is a 16-frame synthetic sequence at 96x96, against the static-convergence decline after frame 6 to 10 (section 7.1).
- **Motion estimator.** Supervised by the exact or RAFT motion (L1 in low-resolution pixels, weight 0.1, weighted by label quality). During the first quarter of each run, samples without motion vectors warp history with the label motion at a probability falling from 1 to 0 (teacher forcing), so the trunk does not learn to distrust history while the estimator is still poor; a CPU smoke run without it validated 14 dB below a model with zero motion.
- **Loss per frame.** The first frame, which has no history, is down-weighted.
  - Charbonnier, plus 0.1 times a gradient loss, plus 0.05 times an L1 loss on Fourier magnitudes.
  - Plus 0.05 times LPIPS on the last frame.
  - Plus 0.5 times a temporal-change loss `|(o_t - W o_{t-1}) - (g_t - W g_{t-1})|` over pixels visible in both frames, weighted by motion quality.
  - Plus 0.5 times distillation toward the teacher (students only; weight 0 from 3 November 2026, section 4.4).
- **Schedule.** Teacher first (both GPUs), then the main 3x student, then the `igpu` 3x and `low` 2x students in parallel (one per GPU), then six ablation runs in three parallel pairs. AdamW, cosine schedule over each run's wall-clock budget, EMA weights, best checkpoint by validation PSNR-Y (from 3 November 2026 the mean over a render copy and a plain-video copy of every validation batch).
- **Ratios.** 3x is primary and 2x is trained. **1.5x is not trained yet.** The pixel-shuffle head needs an integer factor; options (section 14): an integer-factor model with a resampled output, or a fractional head with a learned resampling kernel.

## 8. Frame-generation strategy

- **Two modes, one model.**
  - Interpolation (tau = 0.5; any 0 < tau < 1 from 3 November 2026, for 2x to 6x multi-frame generation) gives the best quality at the cost of one held frame.
  - Extrapolation (tau = 1.5) holds nothing. Its camera prior is re-sampled from the latest input, so camera motion in generated frames responds without waiting for the next rendered frame.
- **Training data.** Synthetic scenes provide exact intermediate flows, disocclusions, HUD with subtitle changes, menus, cuts and rapid camera reversals (20 percent of samples from 3 November 2026, 10 before). Real quads provide RAFT flows. From 3 November 2026 the patch is 256 px (RIFE's crop size), half the synthetic interpolation samples use a random tau in [0.15, 0.85], and a quarter of real interpolation samples are 3x multi-frame generation (frames k and k+3, targets k+1 and k+2).
- **Initialisation and schedule (3 November 2026).** The `high` tier starts from RIFE 4.26 and is fine-tuned first (learning rate 1e-4), then the smaller tiers, initialised with RIFE slices, are distilled from it (4e-4).
- **Robustness.** Engine inputs (motion vectors, depth, camera prior) are dropped at random, so the same model works for engine integrations and post-process integrations. From 3 November 2026 motion vectors are also withheld from half of the real video samples (before, real samples always had RAFT motion vectors, so the network never had to estimate motion on video).
- **Loss.** Charbonnier, plus 0.5 times census, plus 0.1 times LPIPS, plus 0.01 times flow L1 (exact, or RAFT at a quarter of that weight from 3 November 2026), plus 0.2 times UI binary cross-entropy (positive weight 4), plus 0.5 times distillation from the teacher (privileged until 24 October 2026, the fine-tuned `high` tier after).
- **Special cases.**
  - Cuts: the detector forces a frame repeat.
  - Menus and pause screens: the UI mask covers the panel and the newest real frame is shown.
  - Rapid input reversal: measured with and without the camera prior.
  - Low base frame rate: generation becomes less reliable as motion between real frames grows, so integrations should offer a minimum base frame rate (FSR recommends rendering slightly below half the output rate; section 3.1).

**Reading.**

- **Full-resolution flow training fixed most of the `low` tier's failure.** It gained 3.4 dB on Middlebury and 1.2 dB on the ungated Vimeo-90K triplets, and now beats frame averaging on every real plain-video suite. It still trails unchanged RIFE 4.26 by 1.8 dB on Middlebury and 1.9 dB on Vimeo-90K triplets, so at 12.7 GMAC capacity, not flow resolution alone, limits it. The `igpu` tier gained 1.9 dB on Middlebury but not on Vimeo, and stays near frame averaging on Vimeo-1080p.
- **The `high` tier remains the best FG model:** above RIFE on Middlebury (+0.89 dB), on most plain and engine interpolation suites (+0.2 to +0.8 dB), and on 3x multi-frame generation except Sintel. It loses to RIFE on Sintel (0.5 to 0.6 dB) and on static-heavy synthetic interpolation (0.8 to 1.1 dB), and ties on plain TartanAir.
- **Extrapolation is where the system adds most over RIFE:** RIFE cannot extrapolate (never more than 0.6 dB above frame repeat), while the `high` tier beats repeat by 1.9 to 5.6 dB on plain video. With exact motion vectors, plain reprojection is still better on TartanAir (1.8 dB) and, as saved, on Sintel (0.6 dB, 0.1 dB on the 8 samples the cut gate left alone).
- **The camera prior works on reversals** for every tier (+0.8 to +2.7 dB). Every tier then beats frame repeat.
- **The histogram-only cut gate is much better but still not deployable.** Calibrated for 1 percent false alarms on adjacent synthetic pairs, it false-alarms on 9 percent of them in testing and on up to half of the 3x multi-frame real samples, whose frames are three apart.
- **Latency did not move.** cuDNN autotuning left the `low` step at 48.1 ms (47.5 before). `mid` and `high` are faster at 1440p than at 1080p because their flow is halved there, so full-resolution flow dominates their cost.

## 10. Latency strategy

**Definitions** (reported separately in the FG notebook):

- _Game input latency_: input sample to first photons of a **real** frame that reflects it (game logic only advances on real frames).
- _Camera latency_: input sample to the first displayed frame of any kind whose camera reflects it.
- _Generated-frame content age_: time since the newest real input in a displayed generated frame.

**Model** (`simulate_latency`): a GPU-bound loop with just-in-time CPU scheduling (Reflex / Anti-Lag / XeLL style, no queued frames) on a variable-refresh display, with log-normal render-time jitter.

- **Interpolation** holds real frame k until the frame between k-1 and k has been generated and shown. Game latency rises by about half a frame plus the generation time.
- **Extrapolation** holds nothing, so game latency equals native.
- **Late-latched extrapolation** re-samples the camera just before generating. Camera latency of generated frames then drops to about the generation time plus present.

The model is driven by T4-measured generation times; on target hardware these must be replaced with measured times.

**Engineering rules.**

1. Never queue frames: CPU work starts just in time.
2. Run FG on an asynchronous compute queue, with presentation paced on a dedicated thread (as FSR does).
3. Split FG into a per-pair part and a per-frame part when generating several frames (DLSS 4 MFG).
4. Disable FG below a base-rate threshold.
5. Click-to-photon measurement on hardware (PresentMon or an LDAT-like sensor) is planned for the integration phase; Kaggle cannot measure display latency.

**Baselines.**

- TSR: jitter-aware bicubic and bilinear upsampling (both sample at the true jittered positions), and an analytic TAA upscaler (history reprojection, YCoCg 3x3 neighbourhood clamp, 10 percent blend).

## 13. Hardware and runtime strategy

- **Arithmetic.** FP16 everywhere; FP32 only for warp coordinates and losses. INT8 (DP4a-friendly) quantisation-aware fine-tuning is planned for the `igpu` tiers.
- **Export.**
  - The fused networks export to ONNX (opset 17, static shapes per render resolution; `GridSample` needs opset 16+), with ONNX Runtime parity checked in the notebooks.
  - Deployment paths: [DirectML](https://github.com/microsoft/DirectML) on Windows (all vendors), Vulkan compute through [ncnn](https://github.com/Tencent/ncnn) (Windows, Linux, Android), Metal on Apple GPUs (to be evaluated), and TensorRT as the NVIDIA-only fast path.
  - The pre- and post-processing (dilation, reprojection, pixel shuffle, blend) belong in the engine's compute shaders.
- **Memory (estimate, 1080p, TSR 3x).**
  - Two FP16 RGBA history buffers at 1920x1080 take 33.2 MB.
  - TSR activations depend on the tier: the `low` tier's 24-channel ping-pong at 640x360 plus its 48-channel half-resolution level is about 28 MB.
  - FG activations of the redesigned IFNet tiers are `Not measured in the current repository.` (the old figures, 12 to 70 MB, were for the networks of 24 October 2026).
  - The previous plan's 100 MB pipeline budget remains the target.

## 15. Known limitations

1. **No real engine G-buffer data on Kaggle or Hugging Face with open terms** (section 5.2).
   - Lighting-dependent effects (specular aliasing, reflections, shadows, volumetrics, ray-traced noise) are under-represented.
   - The synthetic renderer covers geometry, motion, disocclusion, transparency without motion vectors, and UI only.
2. **Real sequences mostly lack exact motion.** Video motion comes from RAFT and carries its errors.
3. **GameIR frames are 10 rendered frames apart**, so its sequences are effectively high-motion.
4. **Simulated, not real, low-resolution renders.** Jittered single-sample reads of a finished high-resolution frame miss level-of-detail changes, mip selection and shading-rate effects of a real low-resolution render. GameIR native renders test this gap (2x only).
5. **Kaggle limits training** to about 10 hours per notebook and 3.3 hours per stage: models are small and under-trained compared with production systems, and the TSR results no longer improve with longer budgets at this data size (section 7.1); tiers above `mid` are not trained for TSR.
6. **T4 PyTorch latency is not in-engine latency on target GPUs**; the latency model is analytic.
7. **No head-to-head with FSR 3.1, DLSS or XeSS yet** (it needs engine captures with their SDKs).
8. **Perceptual deduplication is keyframe-based** with global thresholds (section 6).
9. **1.5x is not trained.** Fractional ratios need a different output head.
10. **Distillation gains are small or absent.** The trained TSR teacher gave none in 20-minute ablations in two runs (TSR students train without it since 3 November 2026). FG distillation from the fine-tuned `high` tier gave +0.2 to +1.3 dB in the 30-minute ablations of 8 November 2026 (largest on TartanAir extrapolation). The TSR student's history weight still shows a grid at the 3x sub-pixel period (the README's Vid4 figure); whether the output-resolution refinement removed the dot pattern on flat areas is not measured.
11. **Validation is not comparable across ablations.** Each ablation validates on batches built with its own recipe; only the shared test suites compare them.
12. **TSR validation has no exact-motion source.** TartanAir and Sintel have no validation items in the manifest, so checkpoint selection uses Vimeo-90K and REDS windows with RAFT motion.
13. **The FG camera prior is measured only on synthetic reversals.** There it adds 0.8 to 2.7 dB per tier (section 8.1). Its late-latching benefit on real input latency exists only in the pacing model.
14. **T4 eager latency is far above the arithmetic estimate and above every tier budget**: 48.1 ms for the 12.7-GMAC FG `low` tier and 27.7 ms for the TSR `low` tier on plain video, unchanged by cuDNN autotuning in the runs of November 2026. At these times FG lowers the displayed frame rate on a T4 (30 to 24.6 fps in the pacing model). Compute-shader fusion or an optimised runtime (TensorRT, DirectML) is needed before latency claims on any GPU.
15. **Static-scene convergence is fixed for 16 frames, not beyond.** The 16-frame unrolls removed the decline of 24 October 2026 (section 7.1); sequences longer than 16 frames are not tested.
16. **One run per configuration and seed.** Run-to-run variance is not measured formally. The TSR configuration ran twice (2 to 3 and 3 to 4 November 2026, with different budgets) and its test means differed by at most 0.3 dB, so differences of a few tenths of a dB between ablations may be noise.
17. **FG Vimeo-1080p tOF ranks frame averaging first** in three runs, against every image metric; per-clip values are in `temporal_consistency_per_clip.csv` and have not been analysed.
18. **The plain-video cut gate still false-alarms**: 9 percent of continuous synthetic pairs against a 1 percent calibration target, up to 25 percent of two-frame real samples and 30 to 50 percent of 3x multi-frame samples (section 8.1). Its threshold is calibrated on adjacent synthetic pairs only.
19. **The TSR motion estimator has no pre-trained motion.** RIFE's flow toward the earlier frame is untrained at the later frame's time (the blend ignores it there: at t = 1 RIFE 4.26 reported about 13 px for a 3 px shift on CPU), and narrower sliced copies are not motion estimators, so it learns from the exact and RAFT motion labels in the TSR budget alone.
20. **The FG tiers are 3.4x to 6x costlier than before the redesign** (section 4.5), the price of estimating motion from colour. Only `mid` (44 GMAC per 1080p frame) and `high` (77 GMAC) match or beat RIFE 4.26 on ordinary video. The `low` tier (12.7 GMAC) is 1.5 to 5.0 dB below RIFE and `igpu` (9.4 GMAC) is near frame averaging on Vimeo-1080p (section 8.1).
21. **FG plain-video means are reduced by cut-gate false alarms** (limitation 18). The README therefore also reports means on samples the gate left alone; they rest on few samples for Sintel (8), TartanAir (9), Vid4 (4) and the native-1080p suite (5).
22. **Engine motion vectors add little to FG interpolation** (`high` within -0.6 to +0.7 dB of RIFE with them in the run of 8 November 2026); a model that uses them better, or forward splatting along them, is open.
23. **Little FG quality evidence at deployment resolution.** Only the `vimeo1080p_native` suite (6 Vimeo-1080p clips at 1072x1904) runs at 1080p; every other FG suite has at most 544 lines.
24. **Plain-video TSR evaluation uses the standard bicubic degradation**, a clean case; real video adds codec artefacts that training covers only with blur, noise and quantisation.

**Run order on Kaggle** (GPU T4 x2, Internet on):

1. `neural-data-pipeline`: attach the seven Kaggle datasets; this produces the manifest and the Hugging Face subsets.
2. `neural-supersampling` and `neural-frame-generation`: attach the pipeline output and the same Kaggle datasets.

Each notebook can also run alone (inline manifest, smaller caps). `QUICK_RUN = True` gives a short smoke test of every cell.

**If a version shows no logs for a long time.** Kaggle mounts every attached input before the kernel starts, and no cell output exists until it does. A version that ends with `Mount data: ERRORED_MOUNTING_DATASET` for one dataset never ran any code (seen for `daehoyang/flickr2k` in both training notebooks in October 2026, a dataset that mounted normally for the data pipeline on 18 October). Stop the version, remove or re-add that dataset in the inputs and save again; the training notebooks leave out manifest sources whose data is not reachable and record the check in `metrics/reachable_sources.json`.

**Run safeguards in the training notebooks.**

- The library runs on PyTorch 2.0 (the Kaggle image of the data-pipeline run) and later, including 2.11 (the image of the TSR and FG runs of 24 October and November 2026): gradient scaling and ONNX export go through version-independent helpers.
- The run ceiling `RUN_BUDGET_H` is 10.8 hours (Kaggle's 12-hour limit minus a 72-minute margin), and the stage budgets are sized to fill it (10.13 and 10.07 hours measured in the TSR and FG runs of 3 to 4 and 8 November 2026); they shrink automatically when caches take longer.
- Training caches go to `/tmp/nss_cache`, outside the 20 GB saved-output folder. All caches share one decoding budget (`cache.build_min`, 30 minutes: a quarter for stills, three quarters for windows or quads, validation first), so slow data access shrinks the caches instead of the training time.
- Sources of the manifest whose Kaggle dataset is not attached or not mounted are left out with a log line, instead of failing one decode at a time.
- A watchdog kills a training run, with all its DDP processes, once it is 25 percent plus 15 minutes past its budget, so a hung data-loader worker or collective is recorded as a failed run instead of holding the session until Kaggle's limit. Interrupting the cell also kills the run's processes.
- Batch sizes are probed to 75 percent of VRAM, and the notebook releases its cached GPU memory before every training run.
- A run that runs out of GPU memory is retried once with half the batch. Any other failed run is recorded in `metrics/failed_runs.json` and does not stop the notebook: students train without distillation if the teacher failed, and evaluation skips missing models, unreadable clips and methods that fail on one suite.
- A run whose loss stays non-finite for 200 steps on any rank stops with an error and is recorded as failed (the first TSR teacher ran its whole 100 minutes on NaN), and the batch probe reports whether the loss is finite at initialisation.
- RAFT runs at most 1024 px on the longer side (the flow is rescaled), so 1440p clips fit in T4 memory.
- Test clips go through `nss_eval.test_clips`, which skips unreadable and blank clips and, for frame generation, frozen clips and clips with a scene cut or flash, so no suite mean is set by clips every method reconstructs exactly or none can predict.

### 23.2 Next steps, in order of value

1. **FG `low` tier capacity.** Train RIFE's widths at half-resolution flow (38.6 GMAC per 1080p frame, 30.90 dB on Middlebury before training) against the current `low` tier, both distilled from `high`. Target: at least RIFE 4.26 on Middlebury and Vimeo-90K.
2. **Cut gate.** Calibrate the histogram threshold on real continuous clips and per frame gap (1 and 3 frames), or learn the gate. Target: at most 1 percent false alarms on the real suites with every synthetic cut detected.
3. **Runtime.** Time the exported ONNX models with ONNX Runtime GPU or TensorRT on the T4, then DirectML or Vulkan on an integrated and an entry GPU, and fuse the full-resolution warps and blends. This is the largest gap: every tier is above its latency budget.
4. **Variance.** Repeat the main configurations with two more seeds so effects of a few tenths of a dB can be judged.
5. **Data before time.** TSR no longer improves with longer budgets, so the next TSR gains need more exact-motion data (roadmap phase 1) or engine captures (phase 2).
